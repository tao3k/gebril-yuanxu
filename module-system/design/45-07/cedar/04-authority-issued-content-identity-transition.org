* Authority-issued content-identity transition

** Transition-evidence authority requirement

The generation-transition predicate establishes what a compatible advance or
semantic rotation means.  A proof of those equations is not evidence that the
real content-identity authority issued the transition, and an issuer identity
alone does not prove that the claimed generation is the authority's current
head.  Admission therefore MUST bind the transition artifact to all three
owners:

- the exact Cedar content-identity transition authority;
- an authority-issued validation context;
- the trusted current checkpoint and watermark.

The checkpoint context must also commit the exact validated transition
artifact.  Authority identity plus generation is not sufficient: two distinct
semantic rotations can share both coordinates and otherwise reuse the same
checkpoint.

The existing checkpoint contract represents authority identities as =Nat=,
while =CedarInputContentIdentityAuthority= uses =String=.  An implicit or
collision-prone conversion would create another authority-confusion hole.
Their bridge therefore requires an explicitly injective identity encoding.
Likewise, =authorizationSnapshotIdentity= and transition artifact identity
belong to different identity domains.  They MUST be related by an
authority-validated snapshot projection, not collapsed into numerical
equality.

** Transition-evidence counterexamples

The executable model exposes two independent failures:

1. =selfReportedAcceptanceAllowsCounterfeitIssuer= constructs a transition
   claim whose issuer is =999= while the actual authority is =17=; the legacy
   structural predicate still accepts it because issuer identity is absent.
2. =issuerOnlyAcceptanceAllowsNonCurrentHead= uses the correct issuer but
   claims generation =4= while the authority has already reached generation
   =5=; issuer equality alone still accepts the stale transition.
3. =contextOnlyCheckpointAllowsSameGenerationSemanticFork= constructs two
   valid rotations from semantic identity =31= to =41= and =42=.  Both use
   authority =17= and generation =4=, so the same context-only checkpoint
   admits both even though their semantic identities and artifact identities
   differ.
4. =registryProjectionAcceptsSeparatedIdentityDomains= and
   =collapsedIdentityRejectsValidSeparatedProjection= exhibit a valid snapshot
   =900= that resolves to artifact =701=.  Registry projection accepts the
   relation, while the collapsed equality contract incorrectly rejects it.

** Transition-evidence decision

The v1 closure reuses rather than duplicates the established owners:

- =ReceiptAuthorityBindingClosure.AcceptanceAuthority= and
  =AuthorityBoundNode= own exact artifact-to-binding validation;
- =ReceiptContextCheckpointClosure.LiveCheckpointAttestationAuthority= owns
  issued current-head attestations and watermark admission;
- =CedarInputIdentityTransitionAcceptanceAuthority= adds the missing explicit
  String authority identity, validates one global transition-evidence envelope
  across old/current pairs, and binds both authorities to that identity;
- =CedarInputAuthorityIdentityEncoding= requires an injective bridge into the
  checkpoint owner's =Nat= identity domain;
- =AuthorityValidatedSnapshotProjectionClosure= owns the generic deterministic
  snapshot-key-to-payload projection; the transition instance uses payload
  =Nat= for the validated artifact identity;
- the transition binding carries either the real compatible-transition proof
  or the real semantic-rotation proof and binds its context generation to the
  current Cedar authority generation;
- a separately authority-bound projection node resolves the context's
  =authorizationSnapshotIdentity= to the nonzero transition artifact identity;
  the transition validator proves bounded identity uniqueness only over
  artifacts it actually validates.

Thus a compatible equation, a matching issuer label, or a caller-selected
checkpoint is insufficient independently.  The admitted node must be produced
by the exact authority validator and meet the authority-issued current head.

** Transition-evidence verification checklist

- [X] Construct a self-reported transition accepted with a counterfeit issuer.
- [X] Construct a correctly labelled transition accepted at a non-current head.
- [X] Construct two distinct semantic rotations admitted by one context-only
  authority/generation checkpoint.
- [X] Construct a valid authorization-snapshot-to-artifact projection whose
  distinct identities are rejected by direct numerical equality.
- [X] Use one global evidence-envelope validator rather than a validator type
  specialized per old/current transition pair.
- [X] Bind the generic validator to the exact current and prior Cedar
  content-identity authority.
- [X] Require exact =AuthorityBoundNode= validation of the transition artifact.
- [X] Bind the validated transition context to an authority-issued checkpoint
  attestation and its current head.
- [X] Bind the checkpoint identity and minimum generation through a trusted
  watermark.
- [X] Require an injective String-to-Nat authority identity encoding.
- [X] Prove a rejected artifact cannot form an authority-bound transition node.
- [X] Prove an unissued checkpoint cannot close the transition.
- [X] Bind the current checkpoint authorization snapshot to the validated
  transition artifact through an authority-validated projection node.
- [X] Preserve authorization snapshot identity and artifact identity as
  separate domains.
- [X] Prove one current checkpoint selects one unique validated transition
  artifact without assuming global hash injectivity.
- [ ] Implement the runtime artifact validator, identity encoding, and
  checkpoint-attestation producer at their executable owners; Lean proves the
  required contract but does not manufacture those runtime witnesses.
