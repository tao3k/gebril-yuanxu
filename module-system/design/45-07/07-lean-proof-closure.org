* Lean proof closure for the public syntax boundary

** Requirement

The conceptual domain may be called /User Composition/, but the only public
macro head is =use-composition=.  A successful expansion MUST prove all of the
following properties together:

- the outer form is exactly one =use-composition= form;
- the module header is exactly =use-module MODULE as ALIAS=;
- every body clause is either =compose= or =stage=, and stage identifiers are
  unique;
- the admitted plan contains at least one profile operand; an empty body or a
  stage-only body fails with =composition-missing-profile-operand=;
- the generated alias is a hygienic lexical identifier and cannot capture an
  outer binding with the same spelling;
- the alias is presentation identity only and does not enter module semantic
  identity, profile identity, or the resulting POO lineage.

No theorem about the final list of composed profiles is sufficient by itself.
Such a theorem cannot distinguish the canonical macro from a second
=user-composition= wrapper, a name-only alias resolver, or another rejected
surface that happens to produce the same profile list.

** Observation and counterexamples

The earlier generic composition proofs establish fixed-point and effect
closure after a composition object already exists.  They do not own parser
admission or hygienic binding.  Two minimal counterexamples therefore remain
possible under that weaker model:

1. a noncanonical =user-composition= form and the canonical
   =use-composition= form have the same generic module/profile projection;
2. an outer identifier and a generated alias have the same spelling, so a
   string-only resolver reports a match even though their lexical scopes are
   distinct;
3. the former parser accepted an empty body or a stage-only body even though
   the RFC requires at least one profile operand.

The dedicated Lean owner
=AgenticAILabProof.Enterprise.UseCompositionSyntaxClosure= constructs both
counterexamples.  It then models the fail-closed parser and proves that parser
acceptance implies a single canonical =use-composition= outer form.

** Mathematical contract

#+begin_src typst :file ../../../.data/45-07-use-composition-syntax-closure.svg
$ "Accepted"(s) := exists p: "parse"(s) = "ok"(p) $

$ "Accepted"(s) => exists n, m, b:
    s = "use-composition"(n, m, b) $

$ "Accepted"(s) => "profile-operands"("parse"(s)) != () $

$ "spelling"(x) = "spelling"(y) and "scope"(x) != "scope"(y)
    => x != y $

$ "semantic-projection"("rename-alias"(p, a))
    = "semantic-projection"(p) $

$ "generic-projection"("legacy-surface")
    = "generic-projection"("canonical-surface")
    not=> "Accepted"("legacy-surface") $
#+end_src

The last formula is the RFC defect detector: equality after generic profile
projection does not establish public syntax admission.

** Decision

- =use-composition= is the canonical public macro; no second public
  =user-composition= macro is introduced.
- Parser admission, hygienic alias binding, and alias-erased semantic
  projection form one proof boundary.
- Parser admission requires at least one profile operand across the entire
  body; the presence of a =stage= clause cannot satisfy this obligation.
- Generic fixed-point and effect closure consume the admitted semantic
  projection; they do not retroactively prove syntax validity.
- The dedicated proof module is imported by the Enterprise aggregate so a
  future parser/RFC drift fails the formal build.

*** Parser defect and implementation repair

The proof audit found that the original parser returned a plan when its body
was empty and when its body contained stages but no =compose profiles=
operand.  This contradicted the already normative non-empty profile
requirement.  The parser owner now checks its accumulated composed profile
references before constructing =composition-declaration= and raises
=composition-missing-profile-operand= when that set is empty.  Focused Scheme
tests contain separate empty-body and stage-only counterexamples so the two
invalid forms cannot regress independently.
