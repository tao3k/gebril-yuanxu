A Context is a bounded, versioned composition of the information and relations
needed to understand a question or act within its constraints. It may contain
observations, source facts, hypotheses, assumptions, goals, environment state,
decisions, attempts, outcomes, unresolved questions and alternatives. These
retain different epistemic and authority roles. Capturing them together does
not make every item true, accepted or executable.

Short-term Context forms around an actual question, Contribution, attempt or
interaction. Its proposed binding includes the subject/scope, selected source
cut, relevant environment and policy revisions, producer, observation time or
logical position, supporting/challenging relations and unresolved coverage.
Observation time and the event's asserted time remain distinguishable. Missing
or uncertain bindings stay explicit; they cannot be replaced by a fluent
Summary or a later filename timestamp.

This is shared Human/AI engineering Context, not simply an LLM token window.
An Agent prompt is a bounded projection of selected Context, not the semantic
owner or the complete retained graph. Not every captured Context item must be
forced into an assurance Claim or receive a verification obligation; the
domain question determines which acceptance/use requires that evidence.

Formation is demand-led. Query selects an initial slice; an Agent can propose
new relations, hypotheses and counterexamples and expand it within the budget.
There is no mandatory whole-repository ingestion or prescribed next action.
ORG Elements address the source; Contract checks structure/applicability;
domain admission and Governance remain separate from those operations.

Use records what was actually attempted and observed, including failures,
uncertainty and censored/budget-exhausted attempts. Retrieved, displayed,
understood, applied and successful are different observations. Repeated
retrieval alone cannot demonstrate usefulness or justify consolidation.

* Time, environment and value reassessment

Context value is relative to a question and environment, not intrinsic to its
storage age. Keep at least these assessments separate:

| Dimension | Question | Does not imply |
|-----------+----------+----------------|
| Relevance | Does this help the current question or attempt? | Truth or permission |
| Credibility | What source, observations and challenges support it? | Current applicability |
| Applicability | Do the present scope, environment and assumptions fit? | Applicability in another environment |
| Retention value | Is history or future reuse worth preserving? | Inclusion in every Agent prompt |
| Assessment uncertainty | Which premises, coverage or contradictions remain unresolved? | A single confidence score that closes obligations |
| Authority | Is this principal/action permitted at this current cut? | A reusable preference or durable grant |

Elapsed time can reduce the usefulness of a work-state observation without
making its historical record false. A toolchain, target, requirement or support
change can invalidate applicability immediately, even for recent Context.
A stable safety constraint need not decay just because it is old. A historical
failure can gain relevance when the same mechanism reappears. Hence neither a
universal exponential decay curve nor a single TTL is specified here.

"Time entropy" is a design question about increasing uncertainty or lost
association as Context separates from its original time/environment. This RFC
does not equate it with a measured Shannon/thermodynamic entropy, propose an
implemented estimator or claim an established decay law. A future policy must
define the observable inputs, interpretation, uncertainty and evaluation Cases
before using such a quantity. It must not collapse the dimensions above into
one score that deletes history or authorizes effects.

Reassessment is triggered by relevant changes and any explicit review/expiry
policy, not by arbitrary recency sorting. Expired delegation or withdrawn
permission blocks an effect through Governance/Host rules; it is not a soft
relevance penalty. Changes reopen only the affected assurance obligations;
unresolved dependencies remain a frontier. Losing current relevance does not
by itself require rerunning every verifier or declaring a historical result
incorrect. A forgotten or omitted dependency cannot justify scoped reuse.

* Consolidation into long-term Context

Long-term Context is a retained, reusable interpretation: for example a
mechanism, constraint, design decision, procedure or failure pattern. It is not
short-term text kept for longer, a read-count threshold, an embedding match or
an unqualified compressed Summary. Consolidation creates a versioned
abstraction with links to its originating Contexts, actual uses/outcomes,
counterexamples, assumptions and limits. Originals remain attributable.

Proposed conditions for accepting that abstraction are:

1. A named reusable question, subject or mechanism explains its intended value.
   The abstraction distinguishes what can generalize from incidental local
   state, without erasing distinctions that matter to the claim.
2. Source and outcome bindings are retained or their availability gaps stated.
   Successful and failed uses, challenges and alternative explanations remain
   accessible. Evidence kind must fit the claim being made.
3. Scope, environment, assumptions and defeat/review conditions are explicit.
   Supported application in one configuration is not universal applicability.
4. The domain policy's assessment/review obligations are met. Significant risk,
   policy changes and contested assumptions use the appropriate Human decision
   boundary; simple retention must not imply Human approval or authentication.
5. The retained representation preserves enough rationale and provenance for
   future reassessment. Compression fidelity, retrieval/use value and cost are
   evaluation obligations, not presumed benefits of summarization.

Repeated, independently supported uses can strengthen the case, but a fixed
number of uses is not a universal admission rule. A single reviewed safety
constraint or well-attributed counterexample may deserve immediate durable
retention. Conversely, a frequently repeated but unsupported hypothesis stays
a hypothesis. A grant, reservation, temporary exception or current execution
state cannot become durable authority through consolidation.

Long-term Context remains revisable. A new counterexample can restrict scope,
withdraw an interpretation or create alternative hypotheses while retaining
prior observations. Retrieval for a new question rechecks current relevance
and applicability; long-term retention does not put everything into the active
Context or automatically discharge a new obligation.

* Lifecycle relationships and design Cases

These are related activities, not one required Agent route or one enum mixing
duration, credibility and permission. Working Context may be retained without
consolidation; consolidated Context can seed a new working slice; either may
be challenged or become historical-only. Retirement from current use differs
from authorized deletion, which must respect retention/privacy policy and
the dependencies of retained evidence. No deletion is implemented or authorized
by this review.

| Activity | Preserved or produced relation | Boundary |
|----------+--------------------------------+----------|
| Form | Source/observation to situated question and Context revision | Captured is not accepted |
| Use | Context to actual attempt, environment and observed outcome | Retrieval is not application or success |
| Reassess | Changed premise to affected interpretation and obligations | Value loss, invalid evidence and revoked authority differ |
| Consolidate | New scoped abstraction to originating Contexts and defeaters | Summary/age/read count cannot confer durability or truth |
| Retrieve | Retained interpretation to a new question and current assessment | Retention is not current applicability |
| Revise/withdraw | New assessment to old interpretation and retained history | Correction is not silent overwrite or automatic loss of all evidence |

| Case | Positive design expectation | Counterexample/refusal |
|------+-----------------------------+------------------------|
| Short-term work state | Current failure state helps its active investigation | Old process state does not describe a restarted process |
| Time/environment drift | Historical observation remains attributable with reduced current relevance | A recent result on a different target is not current support |
| Stable constraint | Old but applicable constraint remains available | Recency alone cannot suppress a safety requirement |
| Failure-pattern consolidation | Attributed failure yields a scoped counterexample with mechanism/conditions | A failed attempt does not prove every similar path impossible |
| Successful-use consolidation | Checked uses support an abstraction within stated assumptions | Repeated retrieval or copied success prose cannot satisfy use evidence |
| Compression | Retained abstraction links rationale, outcomes and defeats | Dropping a counterexample or source condition blocks acceptance |
| New application | Relevance/applicability are reassessed for the new question | Stored long-term status is not a new verifier outcome or grant |
| Permission expiry | Keep valid observations while blocking unauthorized effects | Authority cannot be refreshed by recall, consolidation or relevance |

These Cases define the shared design expectations. The proof-only projections
below exercise a bounded policy instance, not a delivered Context framework or
measured Context-value benefit.


* Composition and acceptance boundaries

Context identity is anchored in ORG document/Element identity, with distinct
source and Contract revisions. Memory manages temporal assessments, retention,
revision and applicability over that graph. Session supplies consumer identity,
visibility and lineage; a collection of Sessions can select overlapping Context
slices without copying ownership. Removing a consumer or changing its Session
does not change the source Element's identity or erase retained counterexamples.

Existing Memory-core store/catalog/job objects are legacy execution seams only.
They do not define Context, consolidation acceptance or a Session-owned Memory.
The redesign must separate source graph, temporal interpretation, selected
working slice and storage/handoff. Loop-engine may compose per-use-case policy;
the Session collection/orchestrator composes consumption. Public declarations
remain POO values, not a new projection DSL. Their runtime refinement remains
open. Storage success, job completion and checkpoint recovery cannot alone
attest Context usefulness, consolidation quality or permission.

ORG owns Element/Contract/Query source semantics. ASCENT derives declared
relations over admitted observations; Quint checks bounded lifecycle traces;
LeanPOO proves conditional composition and acceptance properties. Applications
such as Aitia bind domain evidence obligations and Governance decisions.
The parent mechanisms must not import Aitia to become usable.

The old session recovery and loop policy documents contain historical runtime
ownership descriptions. For this redesign, semantic selection, relevance,
consolidation assessment and policy composition belong to Scheme/POO; provider
I/O, durable job execution and byte materialization remain explicit handoffs.
This is a design correction, not evidence of a completed implementation move.

* Cross-document composition and consumer-local reassessment

The Context key pairs declared document identity with local ORG Element ID.
The module's length-delimited wire encoding does not use filenames or erase
document qualification. Equal local IDs in two documents remain distinct.
Composition shares exact cuts, but refuses conflicting source/Contract cuts
of the same key. Selecting a revision is explicit; composition is not
last-writer-wins reconciliation or loss of historical observations.

Contract revision is derived from the actual Orgize declaration's bounded
data structure, including query groups, bindings, expectations and diagnostic
fields. It is separate from source revision. A Contract-only revision can
therefore reopen acceptance without inventing a source edit. Equivalent
declaration reorderings may conservatively reopen obligations; this is not
semantic-equivalence checking or source-author authentication.

A composition can be consumed by Session slices with overlapping members.
The module checks cut membership and rejects unknown or duplicate Session
views. Sharing an immutable source Context does not transfer another Session's
actual-use fact, review, local conflict or accepted interpretation. ASCENT
keys those observations by Session and qualified Context key, checks the
current Contract revision, and reopens stale selected interpretations. An
unaffected document/consumer may retain its current assessment.

These slices are structural selections, not security capabilities. Source
admission and Governance must still precede protected source access and
effects. No isolation of processes, protected data disclosure or distributed
orchestration is asserted by a successful getter or relation query.

Proofs imports the Module's composition, revision and consumer semantics.
The added finite =ContextComposition= model covers revision footprints and
Session-local evidence, while LeanPOO's Contract-key patch laws assume complete
dependency declarations. The tuple identity law does not prove the Scheme
string encoding; concrete collision controls check that projection separately.

This is a reviewed Case mapping, not a mechanically proved cross-language
bisimulation. Quint explores finite transitions and deliberately unsafe
mutations. LeanPOO proves conditional laws and uses =ProofObject=,
=withObligation=, =Patch.then=, =pending= and the dependency frame rule. ASCENT
evaluates the declared relation program through its existing Scheme package,
including retraction after source replacement. Its premises do not authenticate
an owner, verify a use outcome or prove causal attribution.

The policy instance has one situated Context, two source/environment cuts,
one elapsed-time event, at most two retrieval observations and one original
actual-use result. This is not a universal utility metric or TTL. A stable
constraint is already reviewed under the declared domain premises; an actual
failure is an attributable observation with scope, not a universal impossibility
claim. New source use evidence must be current, not inherited from old reads
or old outcomes. Counterexample/history retention does not establish current
applicability. Unknown challenge coverage blocks acceptance.

The lifecycle relation program never generates a grant. That is a construction boundary,
not proof of an external Governance/Host authorization implementation. Human
review, deletion policy, provider I/O, compression fidelity and measured
retrieval/use benefit are not executed by this proof Case.

* Cross-Session evidence admission and domain consumers

[[file:../../../../modules/ai-agentic-context/admission.ss][The Module admission owner]] implements the pure protocol. A request binds a
document-qualified Context cut, actual Session-slice membership, destination
Session, scope, Governance Profile identity/revision and a matching Governance
assessment to a domain evidence payload. The constructor rejects mismatched
assessments and out-of-slice Context. A ready Governance assessment is a
precondition, never an authority token.

A configured consumer Profile supplies the current evidence check, permission
check, review adapter and clock. Review seals use the existing Core verification
boundary; copying a presentation object does not copy its private issuance.
The snapshot binds the complete Context cut, destination, scope, Governance
revision, assessed threats and domain-owned canonical evidence projection.
No editable =verified= flag or displayed digest promotes an observation.
Governance identity/revision is a completeness premise for domain policy
changes; arbitrary ontology/policy behavior is not compared for semantic
equivalence. A policy edit must advance that revision. The reopen flag and
relation report a current revalidation obligation, not a persisted history
transition or mutation of an Assurance obligation object.

| Protocol result | Meaning | Does not mean |
|-----------------+---------+---------------|
| =eligible= | Current domain support plus structurally current, ready Governance assessment | Review, disclosure permission or effect authorization |
| =reviewed= | Exact destination/cut/scope review seal is current | Another Session's review applies |
| =permission-current= | The configured domain permission check is current | Context owns or mints an effect grant |
| =admitted= | All three conditions hold for this explicit consumption request | Protected retrieval, reservation or delivery has occurred |
| =obligation-reopened= | This request cannot currently be admitted | Its historical evidence must be erased |

[[file:../../../../packages/lambda-aitia/modules/assurance/context-consumer.ss][AITIA's optional Module consumer adapter]] binds an existing Assurance verification
Host and effect Host to an explicitly configured destination Session, Context
cut and SDLC scope. It checks the real Host seal and grant at review/use,
including private issuance, expiry and revocation. Its payload projection
includes the canonical Assurance subject, seal identity and grant token digest,
so substituting a new grant does not refresh an old review. AITIA imports the
parent protocol; the parent never imports AITIA. RFC 0015 remains a consumer
Case, not the lifecycle owner.

This configured mapping is a domain premise: the local SDLC grant's liveness
does not prove that its principal/action/resource authorizes protected Context
disclosure. That authorization must be checked before provider I/O by the
owning Host. This protocol evaluates already supplied objects and always
returns =effect-authorized=false= and =runtime-executed=false=. It cannot
reserve, deliver, issue or renew a grant. A caller-created Profile or a
successful local policy callback is not evidence of a deployed Cedar policy.
Configured domain checks may call their Host's source reader. That Host must
authorize any such I/O; the Context result's =runtime-executed=false= refers
only to Context-owned storage/effect execution, not the purity of arbitrary
configured callbacks. The local Cases configure no source reader and execute
no provider read or delivery.
Profile owner labels are declared identities, not authenticated Human approval.
The local Cases exercise ready/blocked Governance objects and issued reviews,
not a live Human decision interface or principal authentication.
Destination identity is the collection's declared Session ID. Global identity
authentication and reuse of an ID by a recreated Session are not established
here; an application must bind those authority generations at its Host boundary.

Sharing evidence does not transfer a review or automatically configure another
consumer. Review for Session A refuses Session B; B needs its own configured
mapping and review. A source/Contract/environment or Governance revision
changes the bound review subject. Review expiry or permission withdrawal
reopens the consumer obligation while preserving historical evidence. A fresh
grant requires fresh review; it never revives the revoked prior grant.

ASCENT's =consumerEvidenceAdmitted= joins Module-projected =candidateEvidence=,
=checkedConsumerReview= and =checkedConsumerPermission= by Session/key/source
revision. The projection reevaluates the real checks, rather than accepting an
editable success result. =consumerEvidenceReopened= identifies requested but
unadmitted consumption. These are observations over a supplied cut, not sealed
authority; external callers can still fabricate raw relational inputs. Recheck
the Module/Host boundary at use; ASCENT has no grant-producing rule.

Quint retains its existing finite lifecycle/composition checks; it does not
model Host-private issuance. LeanPOO adds conditional review/permission/epoch
laws, while Scheme exercises the actual Core seals, Assurance Hosts, Governance
objects and real ORG cuts. Neither proof language replaces the Module checks.
[[file:../../../../packages/proofs/context-lifecycle/admission-controls.ss][Proof controls]] only construct Cases and observe Module results, using the
existing AITIA lifecycle fixture to drive configured local callbacks. They do
