* Independent replacement authority

An issued Human source and the recovery authority's binding to it are different
objects. Retiring a source disables its decisions, but cannot authorize its
own replacement. [[file:../../../../modules/ai-agentic-context/installation.ss][Modules installation]] now admits a separately preconfigured
=source-change:= authority when constructing the recovery authority. That exact
authority must share its Governance Profile; it cannot be attached after failure.
Without this DI, replacement fails closed rather than permitting reinitialization.

The source-change authority has an independent actual Core verifier and explicit
host approval operation. [[file:../../../../packages/lambda-aitia/modules/assurance/context-consumer.ss][The Assurance adapter]] checks real evidence/grant before
and after the configured administrative decision. That decision must authenticate
the operator proof and bind the recovery authority, expected generation and
exact target source. It is not a grant derived from the retiring Human source,
an agent's actor label, a same-name source or ordinary business permission.
The local Case uses an exact configured operator-proof object and subject; a
deployed remote operator authentication service is not claimed.

Host-selected source registration remains a trust premise. Another registered
source may be legitimate in its own context, but equal display names do not make
it the currently designated source. Copied sources, approvals, proposals and
Core receipts cannot substitute private issued referents. A no-op replacement
cannot refresh validity or lower the decision cut.

* Binding and retirement protocol

| Subject | Capture and rule |
|---------+------------------|
| Source-change proposal | Recovery authority, expected binding generation, old source/epoch, successor source/epoch, payload and administrative approval |
| Source-change authorization | Actual Core receipt for that issued proposal, bounded by its publication interval |
| Source binding | Exact successor source, incremented binding generation and publication-time issuance cut |
| Recovery proposal | Captured source-binding generation, in addition to its root/install/Human subjects |

Authorization and commit require current Governance, the preconfigured replacement
authority, exact old binding, captured old source epoch and a live successor at
the captured successor epoch. Administrative approval is checked again before
publication and predicates are rechecked after callbacks. A source retiring
between preparation and commit invalidates the captured permit; a new proposal
and approval can be made after retirement. Retired targets cannot be rebound.

Publication uses the existing installation -> Human lock order. Under both
locks it checks old/new source epochs, samples the target's current issuance
cursor, replaces the binding and increments its generation. Old permits and
competing proposals cannot publish another replacement from the captured generation.
Source retirement, recovery publication and binding replacement therefore use
compatible local serialization boundaries. No distributed transaction is claimed.

The replaced source is not globally retired by this operation. Other recovery
authorities may share it. A live-source switch and a switch following explicit
retirement are both supported, but neither revives a retired source object.

* Fresh decisions after publication

Binding a target must not adopt its already issued decisions. A decision is
eligible for the binding only if its private issuance serial is strictly after
the cursor sampled at publication. This excludes decisions minted while the
change was awaiting approval. A new source binding is not an implicit batch
acceptance of its existing evidence or authorization objects.

Every recovery proposal also captures the binding generation. Rotation makes
old proposals and Core authorizations stale even if a live source later returns
in A -> B -> A. The returning source's cut advances to exclude decisions issued
before its rebind, while its actual global lifetime and other consumers remain
unchanged. Fresh decisions still require authenticated events, current scopes,
business approval and real Assurance premises.

These axes remain distinct: source lifetime epoch, binding generation, decision
issuance serial/cut, controller installation generation, source configuration
epoch and Session registry generation. Equal numeric values or equal names do
not identify the same authority. Private issued referents and signed snapshots
bind the actual subjects.

* Obligations and Query boundary

=org-context-recovery-evaluate= reports current binding/eligibility, completion
and reopened obligations for an owned recovery proposal. It never returns an
authorization or an execution permit. Source retirement, changed binding or
an excluded old decision can reopen a pending owned obligation. A foreign
proposal does not become this authority's obligation; an already completed
recovery is not reopened merely because its pending-use predicates are consumed.

A successful source change does not complete a pending controller recovery,
bootstrap a source, renew a review or issue effect authority. A fresh decision,
fresh proposal and fresh recovery authorization are still required. Historical
Project Context is retained. Completed installations are not uninstalled by
binding replacement; explicit root revocation retains that responsibility.

=org-context-recovery-source-state= is trusted host DI inspection of the issued
source referent, binding generation and copied cut; it is not a protected client
Query or a wire disclosure contract. Low-level source-current checks do not
stand in for recovery admission: the latter always reads its private binding
generation/cut, irrespective of caller-supplied diagnostic values. Query/GQL
disclosure and least-authority projection to untrusted clients remain separate.

* Evidence allocation and limits

Quint checks a finite three-source/two-change projection, independent approval,
retirement/captured-permit freshness, old authorizations, prebinding decisions
and retained knowledge. Re-preparation replaces the abstract pending receipt;
Scheme retains distinct concrete proposals and refuses old receipts by identity.
Quint does not establish arbitrary ABA histories or remote source authentication.
LeanPOO Focus proves conditional arbitrary-generation succession, old proposal
refusal and issuance-cut separation; source identifiers represent issued identities,
not display names. Scheme consumes real ORG-derived Context and actual Module,
Core, Governance and Assurance objects, including live switches and four binding
generations with an actual ABA return.

ASCENT continues relational inference over admitted facts, not source registration,
administrative authorization or binding publication. Its existing gate is a
regression; no new source-change rule set is claimed. This local protocol does
not implement durable source history, remote identity transport, multi-process
CAS, protected disclosure, storage executors, bindings or a replacement Memory.
[[file:32-human-source-change-verification.org][10.06.43.32]] records executable results and input identities.
