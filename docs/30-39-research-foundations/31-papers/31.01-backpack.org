* Backpack: interface identity is not instance identity

Primary source: [[https://plv.mpi-sws.org/backpack/][Backpack: Retrofitting Haskell with Interfaces]], POPL 2014.

Backpack studies separately typecheckable packages and elaborates its module
abstraction into the host language's modules and interfaces. The useful design
inspiration is independent interface checking and later linking, not copying its
type theory into POO or pretending that dynamic Contract admission proves the
same theorem as Haskell typechecking.

Our response: retain package/compiler identity independently of rename, and
separate a publication from its effective contextual instance. Dependency
Contracts belong to the boundary. The same source under two effective argument
contexts must not accidentally share context-capturing closures.

Conclusion: replacing source paths with args is insufficient. Control both
rename-preserves-origin and changed-arguments-create-a-distinct-instance. Open
question: which pure context-free helpers can safely share native package caches?

* Issues and Engineering Limitations

Reference: the original paper linked above. Marks: PAPER-INSPIRATION;
DESIGN-INFERENCE; CORRESPONDENCE-NOT-PROVED. No GitHub defect is asserted.
The unresolved engineering question is how separately checked interfaces map to
dynamic, contextual POO instances and their Contract requirements. A shared source
identity cannot justify reusing a closure that captured another instance's args.
This is a limitation of our current correspondence, not a defect in Backpack.
